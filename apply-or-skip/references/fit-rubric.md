# Fit Rubric

Structure the judgment. Do not manufacture numeric precision — the two scores
exist to expose a divergence, not to rank candidates.

## Requirement classification

| Bucket | Test | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| Gate | Unmet = automatic rejection, and no framing changes that | Binary. One failed gate ends the analysis. |
| Core | The daily work of the role is this | Rate evidence; gaps here drive the verdict |
| Signal | Screened for, but learnable or narratable | Feeds the Screen score, rarely the verdict |
| Noise | Boilerplate, wish list, inflated years, duplicated bullets | Discard explicitly and say you discarded it |

Typical gates: work authorization and sponsorship, security clearance,
professional licensure, onsite/hybrid geography, a hard degree or certification
bar, regulated-industry experience where the regulator requires it.

Things frequently mistaken for gates but are not: "10+ years", "BS/MS required"
absent a regulatory reason, a named vendor tool when the category experience is
there, "must have startup experience", industry adjacency.

Years-of-experience lines are Noise until proven otherwise. A requirement for
more years than the technology has existed is a copy-paste artifact and says
something about the req's authorship.

## Evidence ladder

Rate the strongest available evidence per Core requirement. Cite where it comes
from and when it happened.

| Level | Means | Reads as |
|---|---|---|
| Owned | Drove it end to end; accountable for the outcome | Strongest claim; use active ownership verbs |
| Co-led | Shared accountability with a named peer or pod | Honest and durable; survives interrogation |
| Contributed | Delivered a real piece inside someone else's scope | Fine as supporting evidence, weak as a headline |
| Exposure | Used it, evaluated it, was in the room | Never a resume bullet on its own |
| Absent | No evidence | A gap. Do not narrate around it. |

Recency: tooling and platform claims decay in roughly 2-3 years; architecture,
judgment, and domain knowledge decay far more slowly. Weight accordingly, and
name the decay when a Core match rests on work more than five years old.

## The two scores

### Screen (0-10)

What a recruiter, a parser, or a hiring manager scanning for 30 seconds sees.

| Range | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 8-10 | Title, tools, level, and industry line up; the resume reads as written for this req |
| 5-7 | Recognizable but requires interpretation; a careful reader gets there, a fast one may not |
| 3-4 | Real fit is invisible at surface level; needs a referral or a rewrite |
| 0-2 | Reads as a different person applying to the wrong job |

Drivers: literal presence of required terms, title adjacency, level language
(Senior/Staff/Lead consistency), industry or company-stage match, and whether
the top third of the resume speaks to this role at all.

### Substance (0-10)

Whether the user could do the job well, independent of how it reads.

| Range | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 8-10 | Has done the core of this job, at this scope, with owned outcomes |
| 5-7 | Would ramp fast; the depth is adjacent rather than identical |
| 3-4 | One or more Core requirements have no real evidence behind them |
| 0-2 | The job is a different discipline |

### Reading the divergence

| Pattern | Diagnosis | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Screen << Substance | Presentation problem | Rewrite the top third; find a referral. Highest ROI case. |
| Screen ≈ Substance, both high | Genuine fit | Apply now; keep tailoring cheap |
| Screen >> Substance | Will pass the screen, fail the loop | Expensive way to fail. Confirm the user wants it before spending interview cycles. |
| Both low | Not this one | Skip; the effort belongs elsewhere |

## Gap register

For each Core gap, assign one:

- **Closeable** — days to a few weeks of study, and a demonstrable artifact
  exists at the end. Name the artifact.
- **Narratable** — adjacent evidence carries it honestly in an interview. Write
  the one-sentence bridge and check it survives a follow-up question.
- **Structural** — years of a career the user has not had. Do not narrate around
  it. It is either acceptable to the employer or it is not.

Count the structural gaps. One is survivable in a strong application; two
against a competitive req usually is not.

## Posting-quality signals

Weigh before recommending effort.

| Signal | What it suggests |
|---|---|
| Reposted or open 60+ days with no edits | Stalled req, internal candidate, or a pipeline posting |
| "Evergreen", "always hiring", no req ID | Resume collection, not an open seat |
| Level language inconsistent with the comp band | The req was downleveled, or the title is inflated |
| Scope reads like three roles | Under-scoped req; ask what the first six months are actually |
| Sales, GTM, or field title on an engineering track (or the reverse) | Track switch. Easy to enter, hard to reverse — warn explicitly. |
| Comp band spans 2x | The level is genuinely undecided; your evidence sets your offer |

## Sanity tests

1. Which JD line would a hiring manager screen out on, and what answers it?
2. What is the single strongest piece of evidence, and is it in the top third of
   the resume?
3. What claim in the current resume would this JD interrogate hardest?
4. If this application succeeded, what would the first interview stress?
5. Is there a role already in the pipeline where the same hours produce a better
   expected outcome?
6. Does the user want this job, or does it merely look attainable?
